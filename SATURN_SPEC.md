# DAY 2 — SATURN: FIRST-PERSON PLANET ROAST EXPERIENCE

Implement the Saturn exploration section of Day 2 of the COSMIC birthday website.

## Core concept

Saturn is not just a planet being explored. **Saturn itself is the narrator.**

The whole observation sequence should feel like Saturn is talking directly to Richa. It compares its own planetary features with her personality and chaotic habits, in a funny, affectionate way.

**Tone:**
- Dramatic and cosmic
- Funny and teasing, slightly sarcastic
- Cute underneath the roasting
- Occasionally soft and wholesome
- Natural Hinglish, never forced AI-style jokes

**How Saturn talks:**
- Saturn always talks **to** Richa (tum / madam / Richa), never about her in the third person.
- This is playful friendship, **not** a romantic confession.

It should feel like a cinematic encounter with a planet that somehow knows Richa and has opinions about her. It should not feel like static cards or a science presentation.

# SCIENCE PANEL (keep, like Day 1)

Saturn keeps a facts panel and tappable hotspots in the same style as Neptune and Uranus on Day 1. These facts are verified; keep the wording conservative.

**Facts:**

| Icon | Label | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🪐 | Where it lives | 6th planet · ≈ 1.4 billion km from the Sun | about 9.5× Earth's distance |
| ⏳ | One year there | ≈ 29.5 Earth years | |
| ⚡ | One day there | ≈ 10.5 hours | second-fastest spin after Jupiter |
| 🛁 | Density | Lighter than water | on average less dense than water |
| 🌙 | Moons | 270+ known | more than any other planet |

**Hotspots:**

- **The Rings**
  - The main rings stretch about 270,000 km across, but most of them are only about 10 m to 1 km thick.
  - They are mostly water ice, in pieces from dust grains up to house-sized chunks.
  - Roughly every 15 years they line up edge-on with Earth and almost vanish from view. The last time was 2025.
- **The Hexagon**
  - A six-sided jet stream circles Saturn's north pole. Each side is longer than Earth is wide.
  - Voyager first saw it in 1981, and Cassini watched it for years.
- **Titan**
  - Titan is bigger than the planet Mercury and has a thick nitrogen atmosphere.
  - It is the only place besides Earth known to have stable surface liquid: lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane.

**Rule:** do NOT mention Enceladus anywhere before the final reveal.

# INTERACTION, PACING & EXPERIENCE

**Flow:**
- This is a guided exploration, not a slideshow.
- Each chapter has its own cinematic moment and connects naturally to the next.

**Line timing:**
- About 2.5–3.5 seconds per line, longer for long lines.
- Pause about 1 second before each soft line, and longer on comedy beats (the "Giga" line, the final question).

**Controls:**
- Lines auto-advance. A tap or click (or the Space key) shows the next line immediately.
- A small "Skip chapter ›" control is always available.
- Don't force a click after every line.
- Reuse the existing navigation pattern if the project already has one.

**Length:** the full sequence should run about 4–5 minutes at the default pace.

**Camera:** motion is subtle and purposeful. Use lighting, particles and camera moves to guide attention.

# PERFORMANCE & ACCESSIBILITY

**Phones / low quality:**
- Cap particle counts: rings fly-through ≤ 1,500 particles, paper shapes ≤ 12, extra moons ≤ 20.
- No post-processing bloom. Use additive sprites and the existing tone mapping.

**Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion`):
- No camera flights. Each chapter shows as a calm still composition, with its text fading in.

**Text:**
- Readable at 360 px wide.
- Lines wrap nicely (`text-wrap: balance`).
- Nothing is cut off by the safe areas.

**Screen readers:** each chapter title and line goes to a polite live region.

**Fallback:** keep a no-WebGL fallback consistent with the rest of the site.
